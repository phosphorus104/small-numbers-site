How to work out occupancy, nightly rate and net profit for a short-term rental

Platform dashboards show bookings and payouts, but not the three numbers that tell you whether a listing is worth the work: occupancy, average nightly rate and what you actually keep. Here is how to get them from a plain spreadsheet.

The inputs

One row per stay:

  • A: Listing
  • B: Check-in date
  • C: Check-out date
  • D: Room revenue for the whole stay (nightly price times nights, before cleaning fees)
  • E: Payout received

And a short costs table: date, listing, category (cleaning, supplies, utilities, repairs), amount.

Nights are check-out minus check-in: =C2-B2. A guest arriving on the 1st and leaving on the 4th stayed three nights; the check-out day is not a night.

Splitting stays across months

A stay from 28 Sep to 3 Oct belongs to two months. For a month that starts in cell H1 and ends in H2, the nights that fall inside it are:

=MAX(0,MIN(C2,H2+1)-MAX(B2,H1))

That gives 3 nights in September and 2 in October for the stay above. Sum the column for the month to get booked nights, and spread room revenue the same way: =D2*(nights in month)/(C2-B2).

The three numbers

  • Occupancy: booked nights ÷ available nights. Available nights are the days in the month minus nights you blocked for yourself or for repairs.
  • Average nightly rate: room revenue for the month ÷ booked nights.
  • Net: payouts received minus costs for the month.

A worked month

October has 31 nights. You blocked 3 for a family visit, so 28 were available. Guests stayed 19 nights, with 2,470 of room revenue. Payouts came to 2,310 after platform fees, and costs were 670 (cleaning 420, supplies 90, utilities 160).

  • Occupancy: 19 ÷ 28 = 68%
  • Average nightly rate: 2,470 ÷ 19 = 130
  • Net: 2,310 − 670 = 1,640

Mistakes that skew the numbers

  • Counting the check-out day as a night, which inflates occupancy.
  • Counting your own blocked nights as booked, or leaving them in the available nights.
  • Putting a whole stay in the month it started. Long stays across a month end make one month look great and the next look empty.
  • Using room revenue as income. What lands in your bank is the payout; check each one against the booking so a short or missing payout does not slip past.
