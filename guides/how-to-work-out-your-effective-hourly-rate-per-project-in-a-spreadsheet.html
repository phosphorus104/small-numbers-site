How to work out your effective hourly rate per project in a spreadsheet

You quote a job at $3,000, it takes twice as long as planned, and you only find out what it paid you per hour when you add it up in December. A small time log fixes that: one row per stretch of work, and one number per project, its effective hourly rate.

The inputs

A Projects tab, one row per project:

  • A: Project name
  • B: Type (Fixed, Hourly or Retainer)
  • C: Amount (the quote, the hourly rate, or the monthly fee)
  • D: Budget hours (the hours you quoted on; required for Fixed)
  • E: Status (Active or Done)

A Time log tab, one row per stretch of work: date, project, hours (1.5 for an hour and a half), billable Y/N, invoiced Y/N.

The formulas

Billable hours per project, F: =SUMIFS(Log!C:C, Log!B:B, A2, Log!D:D, "Y")

  • Hourly project: the rate is the rate. What matters is unbilled hours: =SUMIFS(Log!C:C, Log!B:B, A2, Log!D:D, "Y", Log!E:E, "N"), times the rate.
  • Retainer: fee × months running, divided by billable hours. Count the current month in part, by days gone or hours used against the monthly budget, so the rate does not jump on the 1st.
  • Fixed and Done: quote ÷ billable hours.
  • Fixed and still running: quote ÷ MAX(budget hours, billable hours). This is the rule people skip, and it matters most.

Leave non-billable hours (admin, quotes, calls) out of the rate, but keep them in the log. Billable hours ÷ all hours is your billable share, a number worth watching on its own.

A worked example

Target rate: $60 an hour.

  • Website rebuild, fixed $3,000, quoted on 40 hours, Done after 52 billable hours: 3,000 ÷ 52 = $57.69. Under target, and the overrun is the whole reason.
  • Logo, fixed $1,200, quoted on 20 hours, 8 hours in so far: 1,200 ÷ MAX(20, 8) = $60. Dividing by the 8 hours would show $150 an hour, a rate the job will never pay unless it really finishes in 8.
  • Hourly client at $75, 30 billable hours, 24 invoiced: the rate is $75, and 6 unbilled hours are $450 you have not asked for yet.
  • Retainer at $1,500 a month, three full months, 18 + 26 + 31 = 75 hours: 4,500 ÷ 75 = $60.

Across all four: (3,000 + 480 + 2,250 + 4,500) ÷ (52 + 8 + 30 + 75) = 10,230 ÷ 165 = $62.00, where the logo's 480 is its $60 rate on the 8 hours worked. Add 20 hours of admin and your billable share is 165 ÷ 185 = 89%.

The overall rate looks fine. The project list says the fixed-price work runs at or under target and the hourly client pays best, with 6 hours still to invoice.

Mistakes that make the numbers lie

  • Typing 90 for 90 minutes. One row and a project's rate collapses; flag any value over 24.
  • A project name with a trailing space. SUMIFS treats it as a different project and those hours vanish.
  • Starting mid-project and logging from today against the full quote. Put in only the part of the quote and the hours still to do.
  • Counting a retainer month with no work as zero. The fee still came in; that month raises the rate.
