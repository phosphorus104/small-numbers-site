How to work out net MRR churn and quick ratio in a spreadsheet

Two numbers tell you more about a small subscription business than total MRR does: net MRR churn and the quick ratio. Both come from five figures you can pull from your billing system each month. Here is how to set them up in a plain spreadsheet.

The five inputs

One row per month, with these columns:

  • B: MRR at the start of the month
  • C: New MRR (from customers who started paying this month)
  • D: Expansion MRR (upgrades and added seats from existing customers)
  • E: Contraction MRR (downgrades from customers who stayed)
  • F: Churned MRR (customers who cancelled)

Keep annual plans in monthly terms: a $1,200 a year plan is $100 of MRR.

The formulas

  • End of month MRR, G: =B2+C2+D2-E2-F2. Next month's start (B3) is this month's end: =G2.
  • Gross MRR churn, H: =(E2+F2)/B2. Revenue lost from existing customers, before upgrades.
  • Net MRR churn, I: =(E2+F2-D2)/B2. The same, after upgrades. A negative number means existing customers grew on their own.
  • Quick ratio, J: =IF(E2+F2=0,"–",(C2+D2)/(E2+F2)). MRR added for every dollar lost.
  • ARR: =G2*12.

A worked month

Start MRR 10,000. New 1,200, expansion 300, contraction 150, churned 400.

  • End MRR: 10,000 + 1,200 + 300 − 150 − 400 = 10,950
  • Gross MRR churn: (150 + 400) ÷ 10,000 = 5.5%
  • Net MRR churn: (150 + 400 − 300) ÷ 10,000 = 2.5%
  • Quick ratio: (1,200 + 300) ÷ (150 + 400) = 2.7

Read them together. Net churn says how leaky the existing base is. The quick ratio says whether new and expansion revenue outruns the leak. A quick ratio of 1 means you are running to stand still.

Mistakes that skew the numbers

  • Dividing by end-of-month MRR. Churn is measured against what you started the month with.
  • Counting a downgrade as a cancelled customer. It belongs in contraction, not churn.
  • Letting the quick ratio divide by zero in a month with no losses. The IF above shows a dash instead.
  • Mixing in one-off charges such as setup fees. MRR is recurring revenue only.
  • Comparing single months. At small numbers one cancellation moves everything, so look at a three-month average too.
