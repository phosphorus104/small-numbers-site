One Monday review for a one-person SaaS: metrics, release notes and the launch list

When you run a subscription product alone, Monday is when three loose ends meet: last month's numbers, what you shipped last week, and what is due before the next launch. Kept in three places, each gets skipped in turn. Here is a 30-minute routine that looks at all three in one sitting, with a worked example.

1. The numbers (10 minutes)

Keep one row per month with nine inputs: new and churned customers; new, expansion, contraction and churned MRR; sales and marketing spend; total expenses; and cash at month end. On the first Monday of a month, add last month's row. On the other Mondays, read the same figures and ask what changed.

  • MRR end = MRR start + new + expansion − contraction − churned
  • Net MRR churn = (contraction + churned − expansion) ÷ MRR start
  • Quick ratio = (new + expansion) ÷ (contraction + churned)
  • CAC = sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers
  • Runway = cash ÷ (total expenses − MRR end)

Worked example: September starts at 8,400 MRR. New 900, expansion 200, contraction 100, churned 350; 6 new customers; spend 1,500; expenses 11,500; cash 60,000.

  • MRR end: 8,400 + 900 + 200 − 100 − 350 = 9,050, up 7.7%; ARR 108,600
  • Net MRR churn: (100 + 350 − 200) ÷ 8,400 = 3.0%
  • Quick ratio: 1,100 ÷ 450 = 2.4
  • CAC: 1,500 ÷ 6 = 250
  • Runway: 60,000 ÷ (11,500 − 9,050) = 24.5 months

Write down one flag and one action. Here: churned MRR (350) is larger than expansion (200), so existing customers shrink the base before any new sale. The action is to read the cancellation reasons this week.

2. What shipped (10 minutes)

List the changes since your last tag and sort them by what a customer would notice: new, improved, fixed. Note anything that needs a migration or a heads-up to existing users. Then write one short paragraph for the changelog and one line of 280 characters or less for an announcement. If the list is empty, that is a finding too: the numbers above came from work done earlier.

3. What is due (10 minutes)

Count the launch list back from the launch date. With a launch on Monday 26 October, a task set 14 days out is due on Monday 12 October and one set 7 days out on Monday 19 October. Sort by due date, mark anything overdue, and pick the three tasks you will finish before next Monday.

The review on one page

Headline (two sentences), the numbers with last month beside them, one flag, what shipped, and the three tasks due. That page is the whole review; file it with the date so next Monday starts from it.
