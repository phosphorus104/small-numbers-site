A month-end money check for a freelancer who also sells sponsorships

If you freelance for clients and also sell sponsorships on a channel or newsletter, your money questions arrive from three directions: who still owes you, which work actually paid, and what to quote the next brand. Here is a 30-minute check for the last working day of the month with a worked example.

1. Who owes you, and what to put aside (10 minutes)

Keep one row per invoice: client, amount, date sent, payment terms and date paid. Three formulas answer the rest.

  • Due date = date sent + payment terms in days
  • Days late = today − due date, only for unpaid invoices past their due date
  • Set-aside = (money received this month − expenses paid this month) × the rate you choose

Example on 31 October: an invoice for 1,800 was due on 30 September and is still unpaid, so it is 31 days late and gets a reminder today. A 3,000 invoice is due on 15 November, so it is not late yet. In October you received 4,600 and paid 600 in expenses; at a 25% set-aside rate you move 1,000 to a separate account.

Then check runway: 9,000 in the bank ÷ 3,000 average monthly spending = 3 months.

The 25% is only an example; the right rate depends on where you live and how you file, so ask an accountant once.

2. What each project paid per hour (10 minutes)

Log hours against projects, then divide what each project earns by the hours it took.

  • Effective hourly rate = project income ÷ hours logged
  • Unbilled hours = hours logged − hours invoiced (hourly work)

Example with a target of 75 per hour:

  • Fixed-price site, 2,400 for 30 hours: 80 per hour, above target.
  • Hourly job at 70, 24 hours invoiced but 26 logged: 1,680 ÷ 26 = 64.62 per hour, and 2 unbilled hours to add to the next invoice.
  • Retainer, 1,500 a month for 25 hours this month: 60 per hour. Either the scope has grown or the fee needs to rise at renewal.

The retainer looked like the safest work, but it paid the least per hour.

3. What to quote the next sponsor (10 minutes)

Price from your own numbers, not a figure someone posted. A cost-per-thousand (CPM) price works for both formats:

  • Video mention = average views per video ÷ 1,000 × CPM
  • Newsletter placement = subscribers × open rate ÷ 1,000 × CPM

Example: your last ten videos average 18,000 views. At a CPM of 25, a mention is 18,000 ÷ 1,000 × 25 = 450. A newsletter of 6,000 subscribers with a 45% open rate reaches 2,700 readers; at a CPM of 40 a placement is 108. The CPMs here are examples, not market rates.

Then compare it with section 2. If the mention takes 6 hours to script, film and report on, 450 ÷ 6 = 75 per hour, right on target. If it takes 9 hours, the price is too low or the deliverable is too big.
