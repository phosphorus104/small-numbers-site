How to work out profit per item after Etsy fees and shipping

Your shop page shows what an item sold for. Your payout shows what landed in your account. Neither tells you what you kept on that item once the materials, the box and the shipping label are paid for. Here is a five-line way to work it out for any item, on Etsy, Shopify or at a market.

1. Start with everything the buyer paid for one order

Revenue per order = units in the order × price per unit + shipping the buyer paid.

Count shipping charged as revenue. The channel usually charges its fees on it, and the label you buy comes out of it, so leaving it out makes the fee share look bigger than it is.

2. Add up what that order cost you

Cost per order = units × materials per unit + packaging + the shipping label you paid + channel fees.

Materials scale with units. Packaging, the label and the fees belong to the whole order, so a two-mug order does not cost two boxes.

3. Read the fees from a real payout, not a fee table

Open a recent payout statement or the order's detail page for an order that looks like the one you are pricing, and add up every fee charged for it: transaction, payment processing, listing or renewal, offsite ads, currency conversion. Use that one total. Fee schedules change and depend on the order total and the buyer's country, so a number copied from a help page is often out of date. If some of your orders come through offsite ads, work out the item twice, with and without that fee.

4. Profit and margin

Profit per order = revenue − cost. Profit per unit = profit per order ÷ units.

Margin = profit ÷ revenue. Fees as % of revenue = fees ÷ revenue.

A worked example: a 12 oz mug at 34.00 with 6.95 shipping charged. Materials 6.40, packaging 1.60, label 7.85, fees from the payout 4.34. Revenue 40.95, cost 20.19, profit 20.76, margin 50.7%, fees 10.6% of revenue.

5. The price you need for a target margin

If you want 55%, keep fees at the same share of revenue and solve for the price:

Revenue needed = (units × materials + packaging + label) ÷ (1 − target margin − fee share).

Price per unit = (revenue needed − shipping charged) ÷ units.

For a planter costing 9.60 in materials, 2.20 packaging and a 9.40 label, with fees at 3.8% of revenue and free shipping, 55% needs 51.46. A 0% target gives your break-even price, here 22.04, which is the floor for any sale or discount code.

What this leaves out

Monthly plan fees, advertising, refunds and the time you spend are not per order. They come out of the month's profit, which is why an item with a healthy margin can still sit in a shop that barely breaks even.
