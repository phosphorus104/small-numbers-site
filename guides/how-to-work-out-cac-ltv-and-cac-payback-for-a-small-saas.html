How to work out CAC, LTV and CAC payback for a small SaaS in a spreadsheet

Once a subscription product has a few dozen paying customers, the question changes from "is anyone paying?" to "is each customer worth what it costs to get them?" Three numbers answer it: customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV) and CAC payback. All three come from figures you already have each month.

The inputs

One row per month:

  • New customers who started paying this month
  • Customers who cancelled this month
  • Customers at the start of the month
  • MRR at the end of the month
  • Sales and marketing spend: ads, sponsorships, tools, contractors, and a fair share of your own time if you pay yourself for it

And one setting: gross margin, the share of revenue left after hosting, payment fees and support. Most small SaaS products sit between 70% and 90%.

The formulas

  • Customers at month end: start + new − cancelled
  • ARPA (average revenue per account): MRR at month end ÷ customers at month end
  • CAC: sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers. If there were no new customers, show a dash rather than dividing by zero.
  • Customer churn: cancelled ÷ customers at the start of the month
  • Expected lifetime in months: 1 ÷ customer churn. In a month with no cancellations, use a cap (say 60 months) so LTV does not run off to infinity.
  • LTV: ARPA × gross margin × expected lifetime
  • LTV : CAC: LTV ÷ CAC
  • CAC payback in months: CAC ÷ (ARPA × gross margin)

A worked month

50 customers at the start, 6 new, 2 cancelled. MRR at month end 10,800. Sales and marketing spend 3,000. Gross margin 80%.

  • Customers at month end: 50 + 6 − 2 = 54
  • ARPA: 10,800 ÷ 54 = 200
  • CAC: 3,000 ÷ 6 = 500
  • Customer churn: 2 ÷ 50 = 4%, so expected lifetime is 1 ÷ 0.04 = 25 months
  • Margin per customer per month: 200 × 0.8 = 160
  • LTV: 160 × 25 = 4,000
  • LTV : CAC: 4,000 ÷ 500 = 8.0
  • CAC payback: 500 ÷ 160 = 3.1 months

A common rule of thumb is an LTV : CAC of at least 3 and a payback under 12 months. Payback is the one to watch when cash is tight: it says how long each new customer keeps you out of pocket.

Mistakes that skew the numbers

  • Using revenue instead of margin. LTV and payback built on full ARPA flatter you by the size of your costs.
  • Counting only paid ads in CAC. Sponsorships, tools and freelance help to win customers belong there too.
  • Using MRR churn for lifetime. Lifetime is about how long customers stay, so it uses customer churn.
  • Reading one month alone. With six new customers, one more or one fewer moves CAC by 15 to 20%. Add up three months of spend and new customers and divide those totals as well.
  • Forgetting the cap. One quiet month with no cancellations would otherwise show an endless LTV.
