How to track job applications and see which sources actually get replies

Sending applications without tracking them is like running ads without looking at the results. A simple sheet shows where your replies come from, which applications need a follow-up, and how far each one got. Here is how to set it up.

The inputs

One row per application:

  • A: Company
  • B: Role
  • C: Source (job board, company site, referral, recruiter, LinkedIn)
  • D: Date applied
  • E: Date of first reply (blank if none)
  • F: Date of first interview (blank if none)
  • G: Date of offer (blank if none)
  • H: Status (Open, Rejected, Withdrawn, Offer)

Dates instead of ticks let you measure how long each stage takes, not just whether it happened.

The formulas

  • Replied, I: =IF(E2<>"",1,0)
  • Days to reply, J: =IF(E2<>"",E2-D2,"")
  • Follow-up due, K: =IF(AND(H2="Open",E2="",TODAY()-D2>=7),"Follow up","")

Then a table with one row per source:

  • Applications: =COUNTIF(C:C,"Referral")
  • Replies: =COUNTIFS(C:C,"Referral",E:E,"<>")
  • Reply rate: replies ÷ applications
  • Interviews: =COUNTIFS(C:C,"Referral",F:F,"<>")

Stage times

With dates in place, =AVERAGEIFS(J:J,C:C,"Referral") gives the average days to reply for each source, and the same pattern on the interview and offer dates shows how long the whole process runs. If most replies arrive within ten days, an application with no answer after three weeks is probably closed; mark it so it stops counting as open.

A worked example

After six weeks you have 40 applications: 28 from job boards, 8 from company sites and 4 referrals. Job boards got 3 replies, company sites 2, referrals 3.

  • Job boards: 3 ÷ 28 = 11%
  • Company sites: 2 ÷ 8 = 25%
  • Referrals: 3 ÷ 4 = 75%

Small numbers, so treat them as a direction, not a verdict. But they suggest that one more referral may be worth more than ten more job-board clicks, and that is where the next week's time should go.

Mistakes that skew the numbers

  • Logging only the applications that went somewhere. Rejections and silence are the data.
  • Counting an automated "we received your application" email as a reply. A reply is a person.
  • Changing the source names as you go. "LinkedIn" and "Linkedin jobs" become two rows in the summary; use a dropdown list.
  • Forgetting follow-ups. A short, polite note a week or so after applying costs little; the follow-up column makes sure it happens.
