How to price a newsletter or YouTube sponsorship from your audience numbers

The first time a brand asks for your rates, it is tempting to name a round number and hope. A better starting point is the arithmetic sponsors already use: cost per thousand impressions, or CPM. Here is how to turn your own numbers into a price you can explain.

Step 1: count real impressions, not followers

Use what one sponsored placement actually reaches.

  • Newsletter: subscribers × average open rate. 8,000 subscribers at a 45% open rate is about 3,600 opens per issue. Use the average of your last five to ten issues, not your best one.
  • YouTube: average views per video in its first 30 days, over your last ten videos. Leave out the one that went viral.
  • Podcast: average downloads per episode at 30 days.

Step 2: pick a CPM you can defend

Price = impressions ÷ 1,000 × CPM.

CPMs vary widely by niche, format and how targeted your audience is. A newsletter read by software buyers can charge far more per thousand than a general entertainment channel. Ask creators in your niche what they charge, look at any public media kits, and choose a number you would be comfortable explaining to a sponsor. You can always raise it once you sell out.

Worked example: 3,600 opens at a $40 CPM is 3,600 ÷ 1,000 × 40 = $144 per issue.

Step 3: adjust for the placement

  • Primary placement (top of the newsletter, a dedicated read near the start of a video) is worth more than a mention further down.
  • A dedicated issue or a full integrated segment is a different product: price it separately.
  • Packages of three or more placements can carry a small discount, which makes planning easier for both sides.
  • Set a minimum fee so small audiences do not end up working for very little per deal.

Step 4: write it down as a rate card

A one-page rate card turns this into something a sponsor can say yes to: audience size and how you measured it, placements with prices, package prices, what is included (links, revisions, a performance report) and your terms. Update the numbers every quarter.

Step 5: track every deal

Log each enquiry, the price you quoted, what was agreed, when it runs, and whether the sponsor came back. After a few deals you will see which placements sell and whether your CPM is too low. Raise prices when you are regularly booked out.
