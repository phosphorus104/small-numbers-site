How to keep a rent roll in a spreadsheet and see who is behind

A bank statement tells you what came in. It does not tell you what should have come in, and that gap is the number a landlord needs. A rent roll lines up rent due against rent received, unit by unit and month by month. Here is how to build one.

The inputs

A Leases table, one row per lease (a renewal at a new rent is a new row, so old months keep the old rent):

  • A: Unit
  • B: Tenant
  • C: Start date
  • D: End date (blank for month-to-month)
  • E: Monthly rent
  • F: Deposit held

A Payments table, one row per payment: date paid, unit, rent month (the first of the month the money is for) and amount.

The formulas

On a Rent roll tab, put units down column A and the first of each month across row 1. Rent received for a unit and month:

=SUMIFS(Payments!D:D,Payments!B:B,$A2,Payments!C:C,B$1)

Rent due is the lease's rent for every month the lease covers. A first month that starts mid-month is usually prorated:

=E2*(EOMONTH(C2,0)-C2+1)/DAY(EOMONTH(C2,0))

Behind = due to date minus received. And to see leases ending soon, with the report date in M1:

=IF(AND(D2<>"",D2>=$M$1,D2<=$M$1+60),"Ends within 60 days","")

A worked example

Report date 15 Oct, rent due on the 1st, roll starting in July.

  • Unit 1, 1,200 a month, lease 1 Dec to 30 Nov. Paid 1,200 for July, August and October, 700 for September. Due July to October 4,800, received 4,300: 500 behind, all of it September. Lease ends within 60 days.
  • Unit 2, 950 a month, month-to-month from 15 Aug. August is 17 of 31 days: 950 × 17 ÷ 31 = 520.97. Paid August and September, nothing yet for October. Due 2,420.97, received 1,470.97: 950 behind.

Total behind: 1,450. Unit 1 needs a renewal talk and a reminder about September; Unit 2 needs a reminder this week.

Mistakes that skew the roll

  • Logging payments by the date paid instead of the rent month. A late September payment made in October then looks like October rent, and September looks unpaid forever.
  • Entering a deposit as rent. Keep deposits on the lease row; they are money you hold, not income.
  • Typing the new rent over the old one at renewal, which rewrites months already collected.
  • Counting a tenant change twice. When one lease ends and another starts in the same month, decide which lease owns that month.
