How to build a launch checklist that counts back from your launch date

Most launch checklists are a list of tasks with no dates, so everything feels equally urgent until the week before. Give each task a number of days before launch instead, and the sheet works out every due date for you. Move the launch, and the whole plan moves with it.

The inputs

Put the launch date in one cell, say B1. Then one row per task:

  • A: Task
  • B: Phase (prepare, build up, launch week, after)
  • C: Days before launch (30, 14, 7, 0 for launch day, negative for follow-ups)
  • D: Owner
  • E: Status (To do, Doing, Done, Skipped)

The formulas

Due date, F:

=$B$1-C2

Flag, G:

=IF(OR(E2="Done",E2="Skipped"),"",IF(F2<TODAY(),"Overdue",IF(F2-TODAY()<=7,"Due soon","")))

Countdown, in one cell at the top:

=B1-TODAY()

Sort or filter by due date and the next things to do are always at the top. A count of tasks by flag, =COUNTIF(G:G,"Overdue"), tells you at a glance whether the plan is slipping.

A worked example

Launch on 10 Nov; today is 20 Oct, so the countdown says 21.

  • Book podcast and newsletter slots, 30 days before: due 11 Oct, still To do. Overdue by 9 days.
  • Write the FAQ page, 25 days before: due 16 Oct, Done.
  • Record the demo video, 21 days before: due today. Due soon.
  • Write the launch email, 7 days before: due 3 Nov. Not flagged yet.

One overdue task, and it is the one with the longest lead time, because other people's calendars fill up. That is the email to send this morning.

After launch day

Keep a small results table: one row per channel, with visits, sign-ups and sales. Sign-up rate is sign-ups ÷ visits. Say your newsletter sent 400 visits and 36 sign-ups (9%) and a social post sent 1,200 visits and 30 sign-ups (2.5%). The post brought more people; the newsletter brought more of the right ones. That is what to repeat next time.

Mistakes that make the plan useless

  • Typing due dates by hand. When the launch moves, every date is wrong, and nobody retypes fifty of them.
  • Marking a skipped task as done. Keep Skipped separate, so you can see what you chose not to do.
  • Leaving the owner blank. A task with no name on it is a task nobody does.
  • Counting only total sign-ups. Without a channel on each number you cannot tell what worked.
